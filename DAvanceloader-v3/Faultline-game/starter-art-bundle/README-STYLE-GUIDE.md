# FAULTLINE Style Guide

FAULTLINE art should feel like fast rooftop action in a dark brutalist city. The style works best when it is simple, sharp, and readable.

## Core Look

- dark concrete
- neon silhouettes
- cyan player identity
- red enemy threat identity
- orange elite warning identity
- simple shapes
- strong outlines
- rain, glow, and motion streaks

## Color Roles

- Cyan means player, safety, checkpoint, shield, aim, or movement.
- Red means danger, enemy, hit, damage, or warning.
- Orange means elite pressure, rare danger, boss prompt, or high-value object.
- Gold means pickup, reward, coin, or special timing.
- Gray means platform, rooftop, background, or structure.

## Shape Language

Player shapes should be athletic and readable. Long limbs and bright outlines help the character stay visible during fast movement.

Enemy shapes should be simple and aggressive. Red enemies can use direct lines and hard angles. Orange enemies can use circles or shield rings to show they are protected.

Pickups should be iconic. A player should understand them without reading text.

## Glow Rules

Glow is part of FAULTLINE, but it should support the silhouette instead of replacing it.

- Use glow on outlines and important effects.
- Keep dark inner shapes so characters do not become blobs.
- Use stronger glow for pickups and rare enemies.
- Use softer glow for background props.

## Readability Rules

- Test every asset on a dark background.
- Test every asset at thumbnail size.
- Keep weapons pointed clearly left or right.
- Avoid tiny labels inside game objects.
- Prefer bold shapes over small decoration.

## Tone

The game can feel intense without becoming messy. Aim for cool, fast, readable, and remixable.
